Recently, thanks to my recent marriage, I have seen very little of my friend Sherlock Holmes. My personal happiness and domestic interests absorbed all my attention, as always happens with a man who has made his own home for the first time, while Holmes, by his gypsy nature, avoided all company. He still lived in our shared apartment on Bakerovskaya Street, pored over his old books and spent his days either in utter apathy, under the influence of narcotic drugs, or in bursts of indomitable energy. In the old way, he showed great interest in the study of crimes, and his outstanding abilities and extraordinary talent helped him find solutions to mysteries that had long been recognized by the police as unsolvable. From time to time, I heard vague rumors about his activities. I knew, for example, about his invitation to Odessa in the case of the murder of a banker; I heard how he explained all the circumstances of the outstanding tragedy of the Atkinson brothers in Trimoneli and how tactfully and successfully completed the mission carried out on behalf of the ruling house in Holland. In general, I knew about the activities of my former friend and roommate, but more than other people who read daily newspapers.
